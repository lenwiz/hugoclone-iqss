## Iris Bohnet

**Albert Pratt Professor of Business and Government, HKS**

**Email:** [iris_bohnet@harvard.edu](mailto:iris_bohnet@harvard.edu)

Iris Bohnet is Professor of Public Policy at the Harvard Kennedy School. She directs the Women and Public Policy Program, serves as associate director of the Harvard Decision Science Laboratory, vice-chair of the Program on Negotiation, and faculty co-chair of the executive program "Global Leadership and Public Policy for the 21st Century" for the World Economic Forum's Young Global Leaders. She is affiliated with the Center for Business and Government, the Center for Public Leadership, the Dubai Initiative, and the Institute for Quantitative Social Science at Harvard. She sits on the Global Agenda Councils on Diversity and Development and the Gender Gap of the World Economic Forum, and serves on boards including the Graduate Institute of International Studies in Geneva and the advisory board of the Vienna University of Economics and Business Administration, along with numerous academic journals in economics, management, negotiation, decision analysis, and law.

She teaches decision-making and negotiation in both degree and executive programs. Beyond Harvard, she has taught, trained, and consulted with private and public sector leaders across the United States, Europe, India, and the Middle East.

As a behavioral economist combining insights from economics and psychology, her research focuses on decision-making and "improving decision-making in organizations and society." She analyzes causes and consequences of trust and its relevance for negotiation. She has developed experimental tools to measure "people's behavior, dispositions and beliefs as accurately as possible" across different regions and pioneered experimental research in the Middle East. Her work has been published widely and discussed in numerous media outlets.

She earned her Ph.D. in Economics from the University of Zurich in 1997, then spent a year as a visiting scholar at UC Berkeley's Haas School of Business. She joined Harvard Kennedy School as assistant professor in 1998 and became full professor in 2006. She is married to Michael Zurcher, has two children, and lives in Newton, Massachusetts.
