## Cynthia Dwork

**Gordon McKay Professor of Computer Science**

Cynthia Dwork "uses theoretical computer science to place societal problems on a firm mathematical foundation." She received the Edsger W. Dijkstra Prize in 2007 for her earliest work establishing the foundations on which fault tolerant systems have been built for a generation (Dwork, Lynch, and Stockmeyer, 1984).

Her cryptography contributions include launching non-malleable cryptography, which studies and remedies failures of cryptographic protocols to compose securely (Dolev, Dwork, and Naor, 1991). She co-invented "the first public-key cryptosystem based on lattices," considered the best current candidate for cryptographic constructions resistant to quantum computers (Ajtai and Dwork, 1997). More recently, she led efforts to establish a mathematical foundation for privacy-preserving data analysis. Central to this work is the invention of Differential Privacy (Dwork, McSherry, Nissim, and Smith, 2006, Dwork 2006), which has generated intense cross-disciplinary activity and received the Theory of Cryptography Conference 2016 Test-of-Time award. With its adoption in Apple's iOS 10 (2016) and Google's Chrome browser (2014), differential privacy began global-scale deployment.

Dwork was educated at Princeton and Cornell. She earned her BSE (with honors) in electrical engineering and computer science at Princeton, where she also received "the Charles Ira Young Award for Excellence in Independent Research, the first woman ever to do so." She earned her MSc and PhD in computer science at Cornell University.

She is a member of the US National Academy of Sciences and the US National Academy of Engineering, and a fellow of the ACM, the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, and the American Philosophical Society.
