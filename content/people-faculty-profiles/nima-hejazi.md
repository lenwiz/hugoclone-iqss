## Nima Hejazi

**Assistant Professor of Biostatistics**

Dr. Hejazi's research combines causal inference and machine learning, aiming to develop "tailored, assumption-lean statistical procedures for efficient and robust inference about scientifically informative" parameters. He is driven by methodological challenges in "robust non- and semi-parametric inference, high-dimensional inference, targeted loss-based estimation, and biased sampling designs." His statistical work is informed by collaboration in clinical trials and computational biology, particularly regarding "vaccine efficacy and drug treatment trials, infectious disease epidemiology, and immunology." He is also interested in high-performance statistical computing and open source software development for reproducible applied statistics.

Before joining Harvard's Department of Biostatistics at the T.H. Chan School of Public Health, he completed an NSF Mathematical Sciences Postdoctoral Research Fellowship, and earned his PhD in Biostatistics plus undergraduate and master's degrees from UC Berkeley. Since 2020, he has collaborated with the Vaccine and Infectious Disease Division of the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center as a core member of the "U.S. Government Biostatistics Response Team and the COVID-19 Prevention Network," working on vaccine safety and efficacy studies for infectious diseases including HIV-1, malaria, and COVID-19.
