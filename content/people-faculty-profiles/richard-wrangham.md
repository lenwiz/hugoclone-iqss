## Richard Wrangham

**Ruth Moore Professor of Biological Anthropology, Emeritus**

Richard Wrangham is the Ruth Moore Professor of Biological Anthropology, Emeritus. He "received a Ph.D. in Zoology from Cambridge University, where he studied under the renowned ethologist Robert Hinde." He served on faculty in departments of Anthropology, Psychology and Biology at institutions including Bristol University, Stanford University, King's College Cambridge, and the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor.

For several decades, he has studied primates in the wild, including baboon and vervet monkey species, but his work comparing chimpanzee and human ecology and behavior has been his greatest contribution. His insights into cultural similarities between humans and chimpanzees—including "our unique tendencies to form murderous alliances and engage in recreational sexual activity"—have profoundly affected how scientists analyze primate behavior.

Beyond his peer-reviewed publications, he authored *Demonic Males: Apes and the Origins of Human Violence* and *Chimpanzee Cultures*, and co-edited *Primate Societies*. He has served as trustee for organizations including the Dian Fossey Gorilla Fund and the Jane Goodall Institute, and chairs the Great Ape World Heritage Species Project. He is president of the International Primatological Society. His awards include the Baron-von-Swaine Award (University of Würzburg, 2000), fellowship in the American Academy of Arts and Sciences (1993), and the Royal Anthropological Institute's Rivers Medal (1993).
