## Michael P. Brenner

**Michael F. Cronin Professor of Applied Mathematics and Applied Physics and Professor of Physics**

**Email:** [brenner@seas.harvard.edu](mailto:brenner@seas.harvard.edu)

His research group uses applied mathematics methods to address scientific and engineering problems. They are described as "first and foremost problem solvers" who search for problems where mathematics can answer scientific questions.

Over the past decade, the research has focused on theoretical modeling in physical sciences and engineering, much of it arising from close contact with experimentalists. Problems addressed include "the breaking of fluid droplets; sonoluminescence (the production of light from small bubbles)" as well as sedimentation of small particles, device design, and electrospinning.

Current physical science projects include microfluidics problems (such as producing small fibers in microfluidic devices), fluid mechanics (like "the aerodynamics of whale flippers"), rheology of colloidal suspensions, self assembly, material science (ion beam sputtering), and atmospheric chemistry work.

Increasingly, their work has moved toward biological questions and phenomena, driven both by the intrinsic interest of the problems and by the belief that "the principles of biology can be used to develop novel directions in engineering." Current projects range from connections between evolution and physiology (primarily in voltage gated ion channels and hemoglobin), to nuclear pore transport mechanisms, to understanding large systems of differential equations in biological modeling.
