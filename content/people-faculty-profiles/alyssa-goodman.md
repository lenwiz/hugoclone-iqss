## Alyssa A. Goodman

**Robert Wheeler Wilson Professor of Applied Astronomy**

Alyssa Goodman holds the Robert Wheeler Willson Professorship of Applied Astronomy at Harvard University, serves as Co-Director for Science at the Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study, and is a Research Associate of the Smithsonian Institution. Her "research and teaching interests span astronomy, data visualization, and online systems for research and education."

In her astronomical work, Goodman and her research group at the Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics "study the dense gas between the stars." They focus on how interstellar gas forms new stars, using observational techniques ranging from X-ray to radio wavelengths. She was Principal Investigator of The COMPLETE Survey of Star-Forming Regions, which mapped three large star-forming regions in our Galaxy. More recently, she and collaborators have focused on using dense filamentary clouds they call "The Bones of the Milky Way" to map galactic structure at high resolution.

In computational efforts, Goodman co-founded The Initiative in Innovative Computing (IIC) at Harvard and directed it from 2005–2008. The initiative created an interdisciplinary center "fostering work at the boundary between computing and science." She currently leads researchers, librarians, and software developers in "Seamless Astronomy," aimed at "developing, refining, and sharing tools that accelerate the pace of scientific research, especially in astronomy." Current projects include glue, Authorea, the ADS All Sky Survey, the Astronomy Dataverse, and the WorldWide Telescope Ambassadors Program. The glue project, enabling "linked-view visualization of high-dimensional data," has drawn her into medical imaging and machine learning research, though its NASA-funded primary purpose supports the James Webb Space Telescope.
