## Isaac Kohane

**Chair, Dept. of Biomedical Informatics, HMS**

Isaac (Zak) Kohane is the inaugural Chair of the Department of Biomedical Informatics and the Marion V. Nelson Professor of Biomedical Informatics at Harvard Medical School. He served as co-author of the Institute of Medicine Report on Precision Medicine that has been the template for national efforts. He "develops and applies computational techniques to address disease at multiple scales" — from healthcare systems functioning as "living laboratories" to functional genomics of neurodevelopment, particularly autism.

Over the last 30 years, his research has been driven by a vision of converting data to knowledge and knowledge to practice more rapidly, enabling biomedical researchers to discover new cures, provide diagnoses, and deliver optimal care. He has "designed and led multiple internationally adopted efforts to 'instrument' the healthcare enterprise for discovery" and to bring innovative decision-making tools to the point of care. New insights from 'omic-scale molecular analyses have inspired him and collaborators to reclassify diseases such as autism, rheumatoid arthritis, and cancers, often using "developmental trajectories of thousands of genes" as tools for understanding complex diseases.

In 1987, Kohane earned his MD/PhD from Boston University, then completed post-doctoral work at Boston Children's Hospital, where he has practiced as a pediatric endocrinologist. He joined Harvard Medical School's faculty in 1992, served as Director of Countway Library (2005–2015) and Co-Director of the Center for Biomedical Informatics during the same period, before it became the Department of Biomedical Informatics in July 2015. He is a member of the Institute of Medicine and the American Society for Clinical Investigation. He has published several hundred papers and authored *Microarrays for an Integrative Genomics* (2003) and *The AI Revolution in Medicine: GPT-4 and Beyond* (2023). He also serves as Editor-in-Chief of *NEJM AI*.

Kohane "is always on the lookout for like-minded 'quants'" who share goals of bringing a better future for medicine and biomedical science to the present.
