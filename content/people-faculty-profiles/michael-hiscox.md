## Michael Hiscox

**Clarence Dillon Professor of International Affairs**

Michael J. Hiscox is the Clarence Dillon Professor of International Affairs at Harvard University. He earned his B. Econ. (First Class Honours) from the University of Sydney in 1989 and his Ph.D in Government from Harvard in 1997. He served as Assistant Professor of Political Science at UC San Diego from 1997 to 2001, then held the John L. Loeb Associate Professor of the Social Sciences position at Harvard from 2001 to 2005.

His research centers on international trade, foreign investment, immigration, development, and private sector standards for ethical and environmentally responsible practices. He has published in leading journals including the *American Political Science Review*, *International Organization*, and the *Journal of Economic History*. He authored *International Trade and Political Conflict* (Princeton University Press, 2002), which won the William H. Riker Prize for best book in political economy that year. His second book, *High Stakes: The Political Economy of U.S. Trade Sanctions, 1950-2000*, was slated for publication in 2009 by Cambridge University Press.

His recent papers have addressed "the measurement of barriers to international trade, attitudes toward trade and immigration among voters" as well as connections between globalization and democratization, and questions about labor and environmental standards and ethical labeling. Current projects include field experiments testing ethical certification and labeling programs in developing countries and consumer demand for ethically labeled products.

Professor Hiscox grew up in Tamworth, a small town in northwestern New South Wales, Australia. He lived six years in Sydney and worked for Macquarie Bank.
