## Malika Zeghal

**Prince Alwaleed Bin Talal Professor in Contemporary Islamic Thought**

Malika Zeghal is the Prince Alwaleed Bin Talal Professor in Contemporary Islamic Thought and Life in the Department of Near Eastern Languages and Civilization at Harvard University. She is also an Affiliated Professor of Government and a member of the Committee on the Study of Religion. Her research centers on the interaction between Islam and politics in the modern Middle East, with particular interest in the formation of modern Muslim states and their religious institutions, as well as the intellectual and political genealogies of Islamist movements. She also studies modern Islamic intellectual history in the Middle East, Europe, and North America.

An alumna of the Ecole Normale Supérieure de la Rue d'Ulm in Paris, she holds a PhD in Political Science from the Institut d'Etudes Politiques de Paris (1994). Before joining Harvard in 2010, she was Associate Professor of the Anthropology and Sociology of Religion at the University of Chicago Divinity School. She is a Member of the Scientific Council of the Tunisian Academy of Sciences, Arts and Letters (Beit al-Hikma).

She has published a study of the Egyptian ulama of al-Azhar since the 1950s (*Gardiens de l'Islam*, Presses de Sciences Po, 1996), a volume on Islam and politics in contemporary Morocco (*Islamism in Morocco*, Markus Wiener, 2008) which won the French Voices-Pen American Center Award, and edited a special issue on contemporary liberal Muslim thought. She is working on a book about Islam and the state in the Arab world in the modern period, forthcoming at Princeton University Press.

With graduate students in NELC, she is building a digital map of Islamic intellectual networks in the Maghrib and Egypt in the 1920s and 1930s called *Afkar*, supported by a Grant from the Lasky-Barajas Dean's Innovation Fund for Digital Arts and Humanities.
