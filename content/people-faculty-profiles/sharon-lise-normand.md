## Sharon-Lise Normand

**S. James Adelstein Professor of Health Care Policy**

Sharon-Lise T. Normand, PhD, holds the S. James Adelstein Professorship of Health Care Policy at Harvard Medical School and is a Professor in Biostatistics at Harvard's School of Public Health. Her research centers on developing statistical methods for health services research, with a primary emphasis on Bayesian approaches. Her work encompasses quality of care assessment, causal inference methods, provider profiling, meta-analysis, and latent variable modeling. She has built an extensive research program analyzing treatment patterns and care quality for cardiovascular disease and mental disorder patients.

Dr. Normand has created analytical approaches for comparing providers through outcomes and process-based measures. Since 2002, she has directed Mass-DAC, the data-coordinating center that collects, analyzes, and reports on care quality for adults discharged after cardiac procedures from Massachusetts hospitals. She and colleagues report annually on hospital and physician performance using prospectively collected medical-record data. She serves as senior statistician in a 3,000-patient Massachusetts trial comparing elective angioplasty effectiveness and safety between hospitals with and without on-site surgery. She also sits on the Executive Operations Committee for the DAPT Study, an FDA-requested randomized clinical trial of 16,000 patients examining appropriate duration of dual anti-clotting medication use after drug-eluting stent implantation.

In mental health, she leads an NIMH-funded study expanding inferences from observational data, working with Harvard School of Public Health investigators on "extending and applying methods" for causal inference in experimental and observational settings with non-commensurate data using likelihood and quasi-likelihood approaches.

She is involved in two medical device safety and effectiveness studies. One is a collaborative effort between Harvard Medical School and the FDA's Center for Devices and Radiographic Health evaluating real-world device and clinical outcomes for hip replacements. A second study, funded by the National Library of Medicine, involves implementing a computerized tool to monitor adverse event rates of new medical devices through continuous surveillance of clinical outcome databases.

Dr. Normand earned her BSc and MSc in statistics from the University of Western Ontario and her PhD in biostatistics from the University of Toronto. She is a fellow of the American Statistical Association and the American College of Cardiology, and an Associate Member of the Society of Thoracic Surgeons. She served as President of the Eastern North American Region of the International Biometrics Society in 2010 and has been a member of two Institute of Medicine Committees: one on Aerospace Medicine and Extreme Environments, and another on Future Directions for National Healthcare Quality and Disparities Reports.
