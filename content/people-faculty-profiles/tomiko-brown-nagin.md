## Tomiko Brown-Nagin

**Daniel P.S. Paul Professor of Constitutional Law; Professor of History, Faculty of Arts and Sciences**

**Email:** [tbrownnagin@law.harvard.edu](mailto:tbrownnagin@law.harvard.edu)

Tomiko Brown-Nagin is described as "an award-winning legal historian and expert in constitutional law and education law and policy." She has published in numerous prestigious journals including the Yale Law Journal, Columbia Law Review, Duke Law Journal, and Journal of Law & Education, covering topics such as equal protection jurisprudence, civil rights law and history, the Affordable Care Act, and education reform.

Her 2011 book, *Courage to Dissent: Atlanta and the Long History of the Civil Rights Movement* (Oxford), received the Bancroft Prize in U.S. History, which the page describes as "the highest honor awarded annually to a work in the field of history." She reviews for several university presses and publishes opinion pieces on education reform. She is a frequent media commentator appearing on NPR, The Wall Street Journal, MSNBC, Time, Slate, The New Republic, PBS, and C-SPAN.

Before Harvard, she held joint law and history appointments at the University of Virginia and Washington University in St. Louis. Prior to academia, she clerked for Judge Robert L. Carter (U.S. District Court, Southern District of New York) and Judge Jane Roth (U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit), and worked as a litigation associate at Paul, Weiss, Rifkind, Wharton & Garrison in New York City.

At the time of this page's publication, she was working on a biography of Constance Baker Motley, described as "the civil rights lawyer, politician, and judge." Brown-Nagin earned a law degree from Yale (where she was an editor of the Yale Law Journal), a doctorate in history from Duke, and a B.A. in history, summa cum laude, from Furman University.
