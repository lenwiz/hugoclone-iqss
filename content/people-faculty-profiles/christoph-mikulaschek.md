## Christoph Mikulaschek

**Assistant Professor, Department of Government**

Christoph Mikulaschek's research interests focus on international security and the political economy of international organizations. He received the APSA Merze Tate Award for the best dissertation in international relations. His work has appeared in the American Journal of Political Science, International Organization, the Journal of Conflict Resolution, and the Review of International Organization. He holds a PhD in political science from Princeton University.

In his book manuscript, he investigates questions about international institutions and power in world politics, including the extent to which minor powers can constrain great powers, whether institutional norms shape state behavior, and what signals international organizations convey when united or divided. He argues that great powers share disproportionately large influence with smaller states to attain unanimity, enhancing the signaling effect of these institutions. His mixed-methods analysis of the UN Security Council tests this argument by examining decision-making, compliance with resolutions, impact on public attitudes, and issue-linkage across institutions, combining "experiments, causal inference based on natural experiments, and qualitative case studies."

A second research stream focuses on political violence, including papers on Iraqi public attitudes toward ISIS, the Iraqi government, and U.S. airstrikes against ISIS using list experiments and original national surveys. An additional article estimates the effect of UN blue helmets on violence against civilians through design-based causal inference and a case study.
