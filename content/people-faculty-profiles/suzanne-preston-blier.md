## Suzanne Preston Blier

**Allen Whitehill Clowes Professor of Fine Arts and of African and African American Studies**

**Email:** [blier@fas.harvard.edu](mailto:blier@fas.harvard.edu)

Suzanne Preston Blier earned her Ph.D. in 1981 from Columbia and holds the Allen Whitehill Clowes Professor of Fine Arts and of African and African American Studies position at Harvard University. She is a historian of African art and architecture working in both the History of Art and Architecture and African and African American Studies Departments, and is a member of the Institute for Quantitative Social Science.

Her first book, *The Anatomy of Architecture: Ontology and Metaphor in Batammaliba Architectural Expression* (Cambridge University Press; paperback, Chicago University Press, 1987) won the Arnold Rubin Prize. Her second book, *African Vodun: Art, Psychology, and Power* (1995) received the Charles Rufus Morey Prize. Other books include *African Royal Art: The Majesty of Form* (1998), *Butabu: Adobe Architecture in West Africa* (2004, NY Times Holiday Selection), and *Art of the Senses: Masterpieces from the William and Bertha Teel Collection* (Editor, 2004). A volume was noted as in press: *Art and Risk in Ancient Yoruba: Ife History, Power and Identity c.1300* (Cambridge University Press).

She served on the College Art Association editorial board as Vice President for Publications, and in 2011 two of her articles were selected for the Centennial Anthology of that journal. She was a member of the "Collège de France International Scientific and Strategic Committee (COSS)" (2011-2013) and on the Board of the Society of Architectural Historians.

Her past fellowships include CASVA (Paul Mellon Senior Fellow, National Gallery of Art), John Simon Guggenheim, the Radcliffe Institute, NEH, the Institute for Advanced Study at Princeton, Fulbright Senior Research, Social Science Research Council, ACLS, the Clark Art Institute, and the Getty Center for the Study of Art.

She is Co-Chair of an Electronic Geo-Spatial Database called Africamap, expanding into Worldmap, where she serves as chair of the Faculty Steering Committee. She co-edited the book series *Worlds of Experience* (with Joseph Miller, Oxford University Press) and *The Image of the Black in African and Asian Art* (Harvard University Press, 2015). Forthcoming books noted include *Imaging African Amazons: The Art of Dahomey Women Warriors* and *Picasso's Demoiselles: Pornography, Primitivism, and Darwin*. A profile on her contributions appeared in the spring 2013 Harvard Graduate School publication *Colloquy*, in an essay titled "Facing African Art."
