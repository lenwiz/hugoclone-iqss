## Emmanuel Akyeampong

**Oppenheimer Faculty Director; Ellen Gurney Professor of History and of African and African American Studies**

**Email:** [akyeamp@fas.harvard.edu](mailto:akyeamp@fas.harvard.edu)

Professor Akyeampong joined Harvard's History faculty after earning his PhD in African History from the University of Virginia in 1993. He received his master's degree at Wake Forest University in 1989, focusing on English labor history, and his bachelor's degree in History and Religions from the University of Ghana at Legon in 1984.

His publications include *Themes in West Africa's History* (2005), which he edited; *Between the Sea and the Lagoon: An Eco-Social History of the Anlo of Southeastern Ghana, 1850 to Recent Times* (2001); and *Drink, Power and Cultural Change: A Social History of Alcohol in Ghana, c. 1800 to Present Times* (1996). He co-edited *The History of Ashanti Kings and the Whole Country Itself and Other Writings* and provided an editor's note for Adu Boahen's *Yaa Asantewaa and the Asante-British War of 1900-01*.

He has received several research fellowships. From 1993-1994, he was the Zora Neale Hurston Fellow at Northwestern University's Institute for Advanced Study and Research in the African Humanities. He became a Corresponding Fellow of the Royal Historical Society in 2002 and was nominated as a Fellow of the Ghana Academy of Arts and Sciences.

In 1998, he delivered the Distinguished Africanist Address at Boston College on "Ghana: Reflections on 40 Years of Independence." At the University of Minnesota at Morris in 1999, he gave the 14th Annual O. Truman Driggs Distinguished Lecture. He was interviewed on NPR's "The World" in March 1998 regarding President Clinton's trip to Africa. He has presented papers at conferences worldwide, including the World Conference Against Racism in Durban, South Africa (August 2001) and the Conference on Dutch-Ghanaian Relations at the Hague (November 2001).

At Harvard, he serves as faculty associate for the Weatherhead Center for International Affairs and as a board member of the Du Bois Institute. As former chair of the Committee on African Studies, he was instrumental in creating the Department of African and African American Studies alongside Professor Gates.
