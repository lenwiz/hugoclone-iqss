## Claudine Gay

**Wilbur A Cowett Professor of Government**

Gay is described as "a leading scholar of political behavior, considering issues of race and politics in America" who has been a professor of Government and of African and African-American Studies at Harvard since 2006. Her research explores how the election of minority officeholders affects citizens' perceptions of government, how neighborhood environments shape racial and political attitudes among Black Americans, the roots of competition and cooperation between minority groups (particularly between Black Americans and Latinos), and the consequences of housing mobility programs for political participation among the poor. She brings these interests to her teaching, including courses on Post-Civil Rights Black Politics, Democratic Citizenship, and Politics of Race, Ethnicity and Immigration. She is "founding chair of the Inequality in America Initiative, a multidisciplinary effort launched in 2017."

Before Harvard, she served as an assistant professor of political science at Stanford (2000–2005) and then as a tenured associate professor (2005–2006). From 1999 to 2000, she was a Visiting Fellow at the Public Policy Institute of California, where she researched voter participation in minority-dominated congressional districts. She earned a B.A. in economics with Honors and Distinction from Stanford, receiving "the Anna Laura Myers Prize for best senior thesis in the department." She earned her PhD at Harvard in 1998, receiving "the Toppan Prize for best dissertation in political science."
