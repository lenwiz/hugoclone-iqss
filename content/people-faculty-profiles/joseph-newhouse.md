## Joseph Newhouse

**John D. MacArthur Professor of Health Policy**

Dr. Newhouse holds the John D. MacArthur Professorship in Health Policy and Management at Harvard University, where he directs the Division of Health Policy Research and Education, chairs the Committee on Higher Degrees in Health Policy, and leads the Interfaculty Initiative in Health Policy. He belongs to the faculties of the Kennedy School of Government, Harvard Medical School, Harvard School of Public Health, and the Faculty of Arts and Sciences, while also serving as a Faculty Research Associate at the National Bureau of Economic Research. He earned both his B.A. and Ph.D. in Economics from Harvard and was a Fulbright Scholar in Germany. He spent his first twenty years at RAND, where he "designed and directed the RAND Health Insurance Experiment, which studied the consequences of different ways of financing medical services." From 1981 to 1985 he headed the RAND Economics Department.

In 1981 he became the founding editor of the Journal of Health Economics. He serves on the editorial board of the New England Journal of Medicine and previously served on the Journal of Economic Perspectives board. Elected to the Institute of Medicine in 1977, he served two terms on its governing Council and won the Adam Yarmolinsky Award in 2009. He is a Fellow of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and of the American Association for the Advancement of Science. He has led multiple professional organizations, including serving as past President of the Association for Health Services Research (now AcademyHealth), the International Health Economics Association, and as inaugural President of the American Society of Health Economists. He has served as vice-chair of the Medicare Payment Advisory Commission, which reviews Medicare payment policy and makes recommendations to Congress, and chaired the predecessor Prospective Payment Assessment Commission.

He received the first David N. Kershaw Award in 1983, "which honors persons under 40 who have made a distinguished contribution to the field of public policy analysis and management." In 1988 he received the Baxter Health Services Research Prize and the Administrator's Citation from the Health Care Financing Administration. His co-authored work won the Article-of-the-Year Award from AHSR in 1989 and from AcademyHealth and ISPOR in 2006, and he received AHSR's Distinguished Investigator Award in 1992. Additional honors include the Hans Sigrist Foundation Prize (1995), the Elizur Wright Award for *Free for All?*, the Walras-Pareto Lectures in Lausanne (1997), the first Zvi Griliches award for *Are Medical Prices Declining?* (2000), the Kenneth J. Arrow Award for *How Does Managed Care Do It?* (2001), and the Paul A. Samuelson Certificate of Excellence for *Pricing the Priceless* (2003).
