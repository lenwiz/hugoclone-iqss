## Dritan Nesho

<strong>Fellow, IQSS; Director, Microsoft Corporation</strong>

Dritan Nesho is a business executive and entrepreneur with experience in corporate strategy, market research, product development, technology, media, and public policy.

He currently serves as Director of Civic Technology and Engagement at Microsoft Corporation, where he co-founded and oversees Microsoft Pulse, a real-time audience and consumer research platform. He is also a co-director of the Harvard-Harris Poll, conducted by the Harvard Center for American Political Studies (CAPS) and The Harris Poll.

Previously, he was Director of Corporate Strategy and Special Projects at Microsoft, reporting to the Chief Strategy Officer and advising on core consumer and business strategy issues. He helped establish a rapid-response strategy "SWAT team" for managing cross-functional, high-value projects for senior leadership. This team is credited with helping drive a 28% increase in market share for Bing, repositioning Microsoft's brand through several campaigns, and the award-winning 2014 Super Bowl ad.

At Microsoft he orchestrated competitive campaigns for Windows, Office, and Bing and led research on consumer, regulatory, privacy, security, and geopolitical issues. He pioneered product-driven media partnerships for Microsoft Pulse with CNN, MSNBC, Telemundo, PBS News, FOX News, and SKY News UK, leading to use by tens of millions during the 2014 and 2016 U.S. election cycles and the 2016 U.K. elections, as well as a branded MSNBC show called "Pulse of America."

Before Microsoft, he served as Vice President and Chief of Staff to the CEO at Penn Schoen Berland, and was Chief of Staff to the global CEO of Burson-Marsteller, helping manage over 80 offices worldwide, 2500 employees, and more than $600M in annual revenues.

He started his career as a political pollster with extensive international campaign experience, conducting public policy polling for political parties in the United States, UK, India, Mexico, Italy, and Southeastern Europe.

He is a fellow at Harvard's Institute for Quantitative Social Sciences (2016–) and has lectured on political polling at Harvard University (2014-2017) and at The George Washington University's Graduate School of Political Management in 2015. He graduated from Harvard with honors in Government and Economics, where he served as research assistant and writer for the late political thinker Samuel P. Huntington.
