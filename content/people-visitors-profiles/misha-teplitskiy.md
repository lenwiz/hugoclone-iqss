## Misha Teplitskiy

<strong>Postdoctoral Fellow, LISH</strong>

Misha Teplitskiy is a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Laboratory for Innovation Science at Harvard (LISH). Previously, he was in the Sociology PhD program at the University of Chicago, where he worked with James Evans to apply computational social science techniques to studying the evaluation, robustness, and diffusion of scientific knowledge. His research bridges Sociology of Science and Management, focusing on how institutional and organizational factors affect scientific discovery and collaborative knowledge production.

Two core themes define his work. The first is resource allocation: how financial and reputational resources are distributed within the scientific community and whether those allocations can be improved. He collaborates with funding agencies and scientific journals to understand how selection processes like peer review "predispose them (often unintentionally) to select particular winners or losers" and how these decisions shape the science that gets done.

The second theme is influence and valuation: whether the value of scientific work can be measured. He uses computational surveys to explore what types of works scientists value, how they influence one another, and whether these influence networks can be identified from the literature. He aims to develop methods for more effectively identifying and directing resources toward important research.

Methodologically, he specializes in computational analysis of large datasets, field experiments, and targeted surveys, applying these tools to data and experimental settings provided by partners including Harvard Medical School, Conquer Cancer, Elsevier, and Public Library of Science.
