Startup Foundry Success Stories

Companies born from the IQSS Startup Foundry

Home › Research Resources › Startup Foundry Success Stories

TeamBirth / Unravel Healthcare

TeamBirth emerged from Ariadne Labs, a joint center of Harvard and Brigham and Women’s Hospital. The program addressed communication failures in childbirth that cause preventable deaths. After a 2019 pilot at four sites that demonstrated significant improvements in maternal and neonatal outcomes, the program scaled to over 200 hospitals nationwide.

Dr. Amber Weiseth transitioned from research scientist to CEO, leading the effort to bring the evidence-based intervention to healthcare systems at scale. The IQSS Startup Foundry provided comprehensive support: mentorship from experienced entrepreneurs, product design workshops to refine the technology platform, data science support for outcomes measurement, an HBS MBA intern to develop the business model, network connections to healthcare executives, and ultimately matched the team with a COO.

After 12 months of intensive development within the Foundry, the venture launched as Unravel Healthcare with sustainable revenue and a clear path to transforming labor and delivery care nationally.

MorphoAI

Founded by Dr. Andy Spielberg from his postdoctoral work at Harvard’s Lewis Lab, MorphoAI uses AI-driven digital twins to accelerate the design of robots, medical devices, and industrial machinery. The technology enables engineers to simulate and optimize complex physical systems orders of magnitude faster than traditional methods.

Spielberg joined the Foundry in October 2023. The program provided customer discovery training to help identify the highest-value applications, mentorship from industry veterans in robotics and manufacturing, an HBS MBA intern to develop go-to-market strategy, and network access to potential enterprise customers.

The results were remarkable: in January 2025, MorphoAI secured a £2.5M UK government grant from ARIA (Advanced Research and Invention Agency). By May 2025, the company was accepted into Y Combinator. Andy recruited a CEO to handle operations while he serves as CTO, and established a UK office. The entire journey from lab to funded startup took 18 months within the program. Visit MorphoAI

terraFlow

Founded by Dan Freeman, a Harvard PhD student in Biology, terraFlow uses AI to accelerate drug discovery by mining scientific literature and pharmaceutical company data to identify biomarkers for drug development. The platform helps pharma companies find biological signals that predict which patients will respond to treatments.

The Foundry helped with customer discovery — connecting Freeman with pharmaceutical executives to validate the problem and refine the product — and pitch development to attract investors. The company closed a $750K pre-seed round including $600K from Underscore VC.

terraFlow launched version 2.0 at the CYTO conference in Edinburgh, piloted with five major pharmaceutical companies (AstraZeneca, Vertex, Regeneron, Boehringer Ingelheim, and Eli Lilly), built a sales pipeline worth $600K+, mined over 100,000 scientific papers, filed two U.S. patents, and even pitched at the HBS Shark Tank event with Kevin O’Leary. Visit terraFlow

Memorious

Co-founded by Spandan Madan (Harvard Computer Science PhD) and Gabriel Kreiman (Harvard Medical School Professor of Neuroscience and AI), Memorious aims to give humans infinite memory using AI. The science powering the company originates from Kreiman’s decades of research on how the brain encodes and retrieves memory.

Memorious builds “Ambient Intelligence” — creating a “Memorome” for each user that organizes all their memories drawn from conversations, documents, photos, and location data. The system resurfaces memories contextually and in real time, mimicking the human brain but with perfect recall.

With backing from the IQSS Startup Foundry, the founders dove into customer discovery, interviewing over 50 potential users including older adults dealing with memory loss, project managers, and AI developers. These insights shaped their consumer iOS app (now in beta) and an enterprise API. The team closed a $3M pre-seed round led by Mayfield Fund with additional top Silicon Valley investors. Visit Memorious

Thresher (now Two Six Technologies M3)

Co-founded in 2015 by Professor Gary King, Thresher was born from groundbreaking IQSS research on Chinese social media censorship (with Patrick Lam, Jennifer Pan, and Molly Roberts) and automated text analysis. While analyzing data from Crimson Hexagon (now Brandwatch), King and collaborators discovered a method to download Chinese social media content before censorship, giving access to the entire corpus of posts Chinese citizens were not allowed to read.

Combined with innovations in automated text analysis, Thresher offered signal-rich data, novel quantitative methods, and world-class qualitative expertise to help government and corporate leaders understand China even when signals had been censored or fabricated.

Unlike many startups, Thresher was bootstrapped and profitable from day one, deliberately declining all outside investment. This independence enabled rapid growth and fidelity to mission. In May 2022, Thresher was acquired by Two Six Technologies (backed by the Carlyle Group) and rebranded as the Media Manipulation Monitor (M3) for detecting foreign disinformation and censorship campaigns at scale.

Neru Health

Founded by Morgan Moncada (Harvard MBA, Blavatnik Healthcare Fellow) with co-founders Catherine Tadina (Stanford, Amazon, Boeing) and Maria Grebenshchikova (HBS D^3 Institute). Initially focused on sleep therapy adherence, the team pivoted during their time in the Foundry after discovering a larger opportunity in chronic care for DME (Durable Medical Equipment) providers.

With the Foundry’s mentorship, Neru reshaped its model from a consumer sleep app to a B2B platform. Their core product, Chronos AI, is a proprietary digital phenotyping engine that uses clinician-trained voice and text AI to deliver 24/7 patient troubleshooting, adherence coaching, and supply management.

Results have been striking: in less than a year, Neru partnered with national and regional DMEs managing tens of thousands of patients, showing 10-20% improvements in medical device adherence, 30%+ fewer human calls, and up to 60% labor cost reduction. The company was accepted into ERA (a top New York accelerator) and raised $4M at a $16M valuation cap.

The IQSS Startup Foundry provides startup resources, support, and networks to social scientists at any Harvard school who are interested in working with an aspiring entrepreneur to explore commercializing research.
